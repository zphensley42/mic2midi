What do the controls do?
Noise gate

Sound below the dashed lines counts as silence. Raise it if room noise makes phantom notes; lower it if quiet humming gets lost.

Smoothing

Averages pitch over a time window, drops notes shorter than it, and bridges the gaps. Higher = fewer, longer, steadier notes. Lower = keeps fast notes.

Pitch snap

Once on a note, your pitch must leave the shaded zone to switch. Higher = wider zone, so wobble and vibrato stay put (but small intended steps may be missed).

Pin lengths + BPM

Snaps note starts to the grid and lengths to the nearest standard value (16th up to whole, dotted too). The minimum sets the finest grid. Set BPM to the speed you hummed at; it is also saved in the .mid.

Tail cutoff

As a whistle dies away its pitch often sags, which can show up as an extra low note. Sound quieter than this % of the note's peak is treated as silence. Raise if you get ghost notes after a note; set 0 to turn off.

Notes: by sound

A new note starts only at silence or a sudden jump in loudness (Re-attack), never because the pitch moved. The pitch of each sound is the median over the whole sound, or just its attack. Use "by pitch change" for slurred humming with no gaps.

Pitch from

Decides which part of a sound sets its pitch. Whole sound: the typical pitch, ignores a wobbly start or drooping end (best for whistling). Attack: the pitch you aimed at when you started, ignoring drift afterwards (best for scatting or sloppy holds).

Re-attack

How much louder than the dip before it a sound must jump to start a new note. Lower = more sensitive (splits soft "da da" repeats, but may split one wobbly-loud note). Higher = fewer splits.
